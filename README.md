# indy-larry

A Discord bot that shows which Indy Center (ZID) facilities are staffed on vNAS. It polls the vNAS controller feed every 30 seconds and keeps one embed per facility up to date in a Discord channel.

[![Build and Deploy](https://github.com/Indy-Center/indy-larry/actions/workflows/build-and-deploy.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/Indy-Center/indy-larry/actions/workflows/build-and-deploy.yml)

## Embeds

One embed per facility (e.g. "Indianapolis ATCT/TRACON is online"), listing each controller with their position, frequency and when they went active. Facility names come straight from vNAS, so they match CRC. The channel is public, so positions read the way pilots say them, in the [Centerline](https://centerline.flyindycenter.com/position) Position "pilots" preset: radio name, frequency and airport, so `IND_E_APP` shows as `Indianapolis Approach · KIND · 124.65` (frequency in mono). Anything the CMS doesn't know shows its callsign alone, with the vNAS frequency (see [Position names](#position-names)). Positions are listed TRACON first, then Tower, Ground and Clearance.

- 🟢 **Online**: at least one controller is on.
- 🟡 **Closing**: every controller on is closing within `CLOSING_MINUTES` (default 15).
- 🔵 **Planned**: nobody's on yet, but a VATSIM ATC booking starts within `PLANNED_HOURS` (default 3).
- 🔴 **Offline**: the facility just closed. The embed is removed after `OFFLINE_MINUTES` (default 30).

### SOP: "Online until"

Controllers add this line to their controller info, with your local time and the zulu time in brackets. Eastern, Central, Mountain and Pacific are understood (ET, CT, MT, PT, or the EST/CDT-style forms):

    Online until 8pm ET (2400z)

The bot marks the position as closing 15 minutes before that time. If both times are given, the zulu one is used. A line without a readable time, such as `Closing at ???`, is ignored. A booked position is also marked closing 15 minutes before its booking ends, even without this line.

### Position names

The status embeds, bookings, "also covering", the closed line, Aircraft on frequency and the busy-frequency alert all use the CMS names, resolved the way Centerline does ([positions lookup](https://centerline.flyindycenter.com/helpers/positions), `resolvePosition`). The bot fetches `cms.flyindycenter.com/api/public/v1/positions` daily and on every restart, and keeps the last copy if the CMS is down (after a restart with the CMS down, callsigns show until it's back). A position is found by its vNAS position id first, then its exact callsign, then the relief rule: one trailing digit dropped from the middle (`IND_E1_APP` is `IND_E_APP`, `IND_831_CTR` is `IND_83_CTR`), else an all-digit middle dropped (`IND_1_GND` is `IND_GND`). Retired positions are never matched by callsign. If the login doesn't resolve, the position's vNAS default callsign is tried. The displayed frequency is the catalogue's, since it can change; the live vNAS one is still what the aircraft count matches on. Frequencies are cut to two decimals, as Centerline does (128.225 shows as 128.22). vNAS and the CMS carry no airport, so it is the position's facility id with a `K` (`IND` is `KIND`); centers have none, and neither does a facility whose id isn't three letters. Short names are no longer shown, since pilots don't use them. Callsigns stay the identity behind the scenes, for sorting, keys and alert memory.

### Message order

In `CHANNEL_ID`, top to bottom: the Iron Mic leaderboard, Aircraft on frequency, the facility embeds, then the airspace congestion embed (if it's in this channel). Discord keeps messages in the order they were posted, so if one is out of place, such as Aircraft on frequency coming back after a failed update, the bot deletes and reposts the embeds in order.

### Aircraft on frequency

With `TRAFFIC_ENABLED=true` the bot also keeps one extra embed in the status channel, "Aircraft on frequency", above the facility embeds and below the Iron Mic leaderboard. It lists every online position (and any secondary frequency a controller is covering) with the number of aircraft tuned to it, busiest first. The per-facility embeds are unchanged.

Counts come from the [VATSIM transceivers feed](https://data.vatsim.net/v3/transceivers-data.json), which has the frequency and location of every radio. An aircraft counts when one of its radios is on the position's frequency and within range of one of the controller's radios, once per aircraft. Range keeps out aircraft elsewhere in the world on the same frequency, and the other airport's ground or tower on a shared frequency. It's `TRAFFIC_RADIUS_CAB` (default 10 nm, for delivery, ground and tower), `TRAFFIC_RADIUS_TRACON` (60) and `TRAFFIC_RADIUS_ENROUTE` (250). Guard (121.500) is never counted. A blank radius setting uses its default. If the feed is down the embed is skipped for that poll and the rest keeps working.

**Busy frequency alert:** when a position's count reaches `HELP_THRESHOLD_CAB`, `HELP_THRESHOLD_TRACON` or `HELP_THRESHOLD_ENROUTE` (0 or blank turns an area off), the bot posts "may need help" in `ALERT_CHANNEL_ID` and pings `HELP_ROLE_ID`. A position alerts once; it can alert again only after its count drops `HELP_HYSTERESIS` (default 2) below the threshold and `HELP_COOLDOWN_MIN` (default 15) minutes have passed. People opt in with the **Busy frequency** button in the notification panels, which adds or removes `HELP_ROLE_ID`. This needs `PANEL_CHANNEL_ID`.

### Airspace congestion

With `CONGESTION_ENABLED=true` the bot keeps an embed with the airborne aircraft (groundspeed 50 kt or more) in the ARTCC (`ARTCC_IDS`) and in each of its TRACONs, from the VATSIM pilot feed. It also counts how many are projected to enter within `CONGESTION_LOOKAHEAD_MIN` (default 15) minutes on their current heading and groundspeed. The ARTCC line is split at FL240 into high and low. Each area shows who's working it: `N on`, `top-down` (covered from above) or `nobody on`. `CONGESTION_TRACONS` (e.g. `IND,SDF,CVG`) limits the TRACONs shown; blank shows every one in the ARTCC. Boundaries come from the [VAT-Spy Data Project](https://github.com/vatsimnetwork/vatspy-data-project) (the ARTCC) and the [SimAware TRACON Project](https://github.com/vatsimnetwork/simaware-tracon-project), fetched daily with the last copy kept if they can't be reached.

The embed goes under the facility embeds in `CHANNEL_ID`, or is its own edited message in `CONGESTION_CHANNEL_ID`.

**Congestion alert:** `CONGESTION_ALERT` picks when it posts, and defaults to `off`:

- `uncovered`: the area is busy and nobody is on it (a center above counts as covering a TRACON).
- `overloaded`: the area is busy per controller working it.
- `both`.

"Busy" is aircraft in plus entering reaching `CONGESTION_THRESHOLD_ENROUTE` (default 25) or `CONGESTION_THRESHOLD_TRACON` (default 10); 0 turns an area off. Like the busy-frequency alert, an area alerts once, then again only after its count drops `CONGESTION_HYSTERESIS` (default 3) below the threshold and `CONGESTION_COOLDOWN_MIN` (default 30) minutes have passed. The alert goes to `CONGESTION_ALERT_CHANNEL_ID` (or `ALERT_CHANNEL_ID`, and never `CHANNEL_ID`) and pings `CONGESTION_ROLE_ID` if set. The alert memory, and the embed's message ID when it's in its own channel, are in `congestion.json`. It isn't committed.

## Notification panels

If `PANEL_CHANNEL_ID` is set, the bot also keeps up to four panels in that channel, in this order from the top. Relief is last because the mobile app opens a channel at its newest message. Each uses Discord's newer message components, and the bot's replies are visible only to the person who picked or pressed something.

- **Iron Mic Notification Preference**: one button that adds or removes `IRON_MIC_ROLE_ID`.
- **Busy Frequency Alerts**: one button that adds or removes `HELP_ROLE_ID`, if set (see Aircraft on frequency).
- **Controller Break/Staffing Notification System**: one drop-down per area (CAB, TRACON, ENROUTE); pick Break or Staffing on your area. A form asks for the position you need relief from and the Zulu time (four digits, like 0200; the form shows the current Zulu time) you can stay on until (Break), or the position you're working and a brief optional reason (Staffing), plus who to notify out of that area's roles. An area with a single role skips that question and always pings it. The request is posted in `ALERT_CHANNEL_ID` and pings the chosen roles. Anyone but the requester can press **Claim** on it, which turns it green and shows who has it; only they can press **Unclaim** to reopen it. The requester can **Cancel** it. An alert nobody has claimed turns grey as Expired at the Zulu time given on a Break request, or after `REQUEST_EXPIRE_MINUTES` (default 60; 0 turns this off) on a Staffing request, and a claimed one loses its buttons then.
- **Controller Relief Notification**: one drop-down per position. Pick Permanent, or Temporary and enter a whole number of hours from 1 to 24 in the form, to get that position's role. Temporary roles come off within 30 seconds of running out, even across restarts. Picking Temporary again sets a new time from now, and switching between Temporary and Permanent changes the role over; Opt out, or Permanent when you already have it, removes the role.

The positions and their roles come from `RELIEF_ROLES`, e.g. `S Ground:111,A Ground:222,S Local:333,A Local:444,T Radar:555,E Radar:666`, so a dev server and the production server can use different roles. The drop-downs follow that order. Each request area's roles come from `CAB_ROLES`, `TRACON_ROLES` and `ENROUTE_ROLES` in the same format, e.g. `CAB_ROLES=S-GC:111,A-GC:222,S-LC:333,A-LC:444`; they can be the same roles as `RELIEF_ROLES` under different names. Each panel is redrawn after a pick, so the drop-downs go back to showing the position names. The panels are edited in place on every start, so a changed setting shows up after a restart.

The bot needs **Manage Roles**, and its own role has to sit above every role it hands out. To ping a role it also needs **Mention @everyone, @here and All Roles** in the alert channel, or the role has to allow anyone to mention it. `notify.json` holds the panel message IDs, who has which role until when, and the request alerts that can still be claimed or cancelled. It isn't committed.

## Iron Mic leaderboard

Staff run `/ironmic` in any channel except `CHANNEL_ID` to keep a leaderboard of how long positions at one or more facilities were staffed. The leaderboard is one message at the top of `CHANNEL_ID`, above the status embeds, with an embed per position in the order Center, Approach, Local, Ground. Set `IRON_MIC_CHANNEL_ID` to post it somewhere else instead, such as a test channel.

- `/ironmic start local:LEX approach:LEX` posts the leaderboard (reposting the status embeds below it) and pings `IRON_MIC_ROLE_ID`, if set, in the channel it was run in. The facility list comes from the vNAS ARTCC data (towers and TRACONs in `ARTCC_IDS`). Each of `center`, `approach`, `local` and `ground` is its own option holding the facility to track it at; fill in any combination (at least one). Positions can be at different facilities, e.g. `local:SDF ground:IND approach:CVG`. Add `ping:False` to skip the role ping, e.g. when restarting one to change its positions.
- `/ironmic end` stops it. The leaderboard shows the final totals within a minute.
- `/ironmic clear` deletes the leaderboard and its log, after `end`. Only one Iron Mic runs at a time.

Totals come from [vNAS Stats](https://vnas-stats.com) ([source](https://github.com/kengreim/vnas-stats)), fetched every 5 minutes. An Iron Mic always counts from midnight UTC on the 1st of the month `start` was run in, so it matches the site's month view. While it runs, the leaderboard's footer shows the whole hours left in that month in Zulu, e.g. `371h left in the month (until 0000Z Nov 1)`. It counts active controllers and groups time by callsign prefix and suffix, ignoring the middle part, so `LEX_APP` and `LEX_N_APP` are both LEX approach and two controllers on it at once count once. Which callsigns a position covers comes from the vNAS ARTCC data: Center is the ARTCC's (`IND_CTR`), Approach is the TRACON over the facility (DAY's is CMH's, `CMH_APP` + `DAY_APP`), and Local and Ground are the facility's own. A position with several callsigns adds them up. `start` refuses a position the facility doesn't have, like PKB approach. Its list only has the network's top callsigns; a position that didn't make it shows as "Under" the last one listed.

Each embed shows the position's staffed time, its Uptime, and its rank among the same kind of position on the network (towers against towers, approaches against approaches), with the gap to the callsign just above it, or its lead over the one below at #1. While the Iron Mic runs, that callsign gets a 🟢 when someone is actively controlling it on the vNAS feed right now, so you can see who you're chasing or holding off. The same 🟢 shows on the position's own embed as "On frequency now" while one of its callsigns is staffed, so you can see at a glance that your Iron Mic position is covered. The embeds are grey, which no status embed uses. The circles go away once the Iron Mic has ended.

By default only members with **Manage Server** see the command; change who can use it under **Server Settings → Integrations → Larry**. The bot needs **View Channel**, **Send Messages** and **Embed Links** where it's run and in the leaderboard's channel, and the role ping needs the same mention permission as the request alerts. `ironmic.json` holds the running Iron Mic and its message ID, so a restart edits the same message. It isn't committed.

## Sending messages from other apps

Other Indy Center Workers can send Discord messages as Larry instead of keeping their own webhooks: to a channel by name, or as a DM to a user. A small Cloudflare Worker in `worker/` (`indy-larry`) does the sending with Larry's token, through Discord's REST API, so the bot on the VPS isn't involved. Following the [RPC vs Queue pattern](https://tech.flyindycenter.com/patterns/rpc-vs-queue/), callers reach it over a service binding, not HTTP. Its one public route is for larry's own bot (see [Role sync](#role-sync)).

```jsonc
// caller's wrangler.jsonc
"services": [{ "binding": "LARRY", "service": "indy-larry" }]
```

```ts
import type { LarryBinding } from '@indy-center/indy-larry-worker'; // npm install @indy-center/indy-larry-worker

// RPC: sends now and returns { channelId, messageId }; throws if Discord refuses it.
await env.LARRY.send({
  channel: 'events',
  content: `<@${userId}> your session is confirmed`,
  embeds: [{ title: 'FNO', color: 0x5865f2 }],
});
await env.LARRY.sendDirect({ userId, content: 'Your training session starts in an hour.' });

// Queue: returns once queued; delivery retries rate limits and Discord outages.
await env.LARRY.enqueue({ channel: 'events', content: 'FNO starts in one hour! @everyone', allowedMentions: { parse: ['everyone'] } });
await env.LARRY.enqueueDirect({ userId, embeds: [{ title: 'Request approved' }] });

// A link button under the message, and changing the message later. send() returns the ID to change it by.
const { messageId } = await env.LARRY.send({
  channel: 'events',
  embeds: [{ title: 'Rating exam', fields: [{ name: 'Status', value: 'Waiting for an examiner' }] }],
  buttons: [{ label: 'Open Teach', url: 'https://training.flyindycenter.com/teach' }],
});
await env.LARRY.enqueueEdit({ channel: 'events', messageId, embeds: [{ title: 'Rating exam', fields: [{ name: 'Status', value: 'Claimed' }] }] });
await env.LARRY.enqueueDelete({ channel: 'events', messageId }); // once it has nothing left to say
```

- **Channels**: callers name a channel from `SEND_CHANNELS` (`name:channelId`, like `RELIEF_ROLES`), e.g. `SEND_CHANNELS=events:111,training:222`. Any other name, or a channel ID, is refused, so a channel moves by changing the setting and redeploying, with no change to the callers. The deploy fails on a malformed entry, a repeated name, or `CHANNEL_ID`, where the status loop would delete the message.
- **DMs** take the user's Discord ID. The user has to share a server with Larry and accept DMs from it; if not, `sendDirect()` throws and a queued DM is dropped.
- **Mentions**: `allowedMentions` goes to Discord as `allowed_mentions`, unchanged. Without it, the users mentioned in the content (`<@id>`) are pinged and nobody else is.
- **Buttons**: `buttons` puts up to 5 link buttons under a message, each opening an http(s) address. There are no buttons that do something when pressed: nothing in this Worker listens for a press.
- **Edits**: `editMessage()` changes a message Larry posted in a `SEND_CHANNELS` channel now; `enqueueEdit()` queues the change. What is given replaces what the message had, and `content` or `embeds` left out stays as it was. `buttons` left out keeps the buttons; `[]` removes them. An edit never pings. An edit to a message that has been deleted throws from `editMessage()` and is dropped from the queue.
- **Deleting**: `deleteMessage()` removes a message Larry posted in a `SEND_CHANNELS` channel now; `enqueueDelete()` queues it. It cannot be undone. A message that is already gone counts as deleted.
- **Checks**: every method throws straight away for an unknown channel, a bad user ID, an empty message, more than 2000 characters or more than 10 embeds; the queue methods queue nothing then.
- **Errors**: whatever a method throws, a refused request included, is also posted to `#larry-errors` (see [Error reports](#error-reports)), so a caller that doesn't check its errors still gets noticed.
- **Delivery**: `send()` waits out a rate limit of 5 seconds or less once, then throws. The queue (`larry-messages`) waits for Discord's `retry_after` on a rate limit, backs off on 5xx and network errors, and drops a message Discord refuses (4xx), logging why. When the refusal is Larry's own setup, the queue run also **fails**, so it shows as an error on the Worker's dashboard: a channel Larry can't see or post in (403), one that no longer exists (404), or a bad token (401). The message is still dropped, not retried. A bad embed or a user who won't take DMs is logged only. After 5 attempts a message moves to `larry-messages-dlq`. Each queued message carries a nonce, so a retry can't post twice.

The bot needs **View Channel**, **Send Messages** and **Embed Links** in each channel in `SEND_CHANNELS`.

## Error reports

Larry reports on himself in `ERROR_CHANNEL_ID` (`#larry-errors`). Both halves post there, as Larry: the bot on the VPS (`src/errorReport.js`) and the send Worker (`worker/src/errors.ts`). Leave it unset and nothing is sent.

- **Up and down**, like a status monitor. The bot posts `🔴 Larry bot is down: stopped for a deploy or restart` when it's stopped, `🔴 Larry bot is down: crashed` with the error when it crashes, and `🟢 Larry bot is back up after 2 min` once it has logged in again. When Larry is killed without warning (out of memory, the VPS losing power) there's no 🔴. The next 🟢 says so, and times the outage from a heartbeat the bot writes to `uptime.json` every minute.
- **Bot errors**: every `console.error`, plus uncaught exceptions and unhandled rejections. Those two still stop the bot, as Node would, and Docker restarts it. Errors from one burst go out as one message, tagged `Larry bot` and the commit.
- **Worker errors**: whatever an RPC method throws, including a caller's refused request; a failed role sync cron run, role-sync batch or bot events request; a queued message dropped because Discord refused it; and a queued message on its last try before it goes to `larry-messages-dlq`. Each call's errors go out as one message after the call returns, so callers never wait on a report.
- **Noise limits**: the same error (numbers and timestamps ignored) is posted once per 15 minutes on the bot and 10 on the Worker; repeats are counted on its next report. A crash that happens on every start is posted once, not on every restart. The bot posts at most 30 error messages an hour; past that, reports are counted and the count goes out once there's room. Reports never ping anyone, even if the error's text contains `@everyone`.
- **Never in the way**: a report goes straight to Discord's REST API, so it works before login and while the gateway is down. It waits out rate limits and retries outages. If Discord still won't take it (wrong channel ID, missing permissions), the reason goes to the container log or the Worker's logs, and the report is dropped. Reporting never throws into the code it watches.

Make the channel private to staff, and give the bot **View Channel** and **Send Messages** there. It must not be `CHANNEL_ID`: the status loop deletes every other message the bot has in that channel, so the bot turns reporting off and logs why if they're the same.

### Roles and channels

Two more things callers can ask Larry to keep in step, separately: who holds a role, and private channels under a category. Neither knows what it is for. Training-tools uses both together (a role per teacher, held by the teacher and their students, and a channel only that role and the training admins see), but an app that only needs roles uses only `syncRoles()`.

```ts
// Roles: who holds which.
const { roles } = await env.LARRY.syncRoles({
  roles: [{ key: 'JR', name: 'JR', members: [teacherId, studentId], exclusive: true }],
  dryRun: true, // report what would change, and change nothing
});

// Channels: find or create, under a category Larry is allowed to use.
const { channels } = await env.LARRY.syncChannels({
  channels: [{ key: 'JR', category: 'training', name: 'Jo Rivera', visibleTo: [roles[0].roleId, trainingAdminRoleId] }],
});

// Post to one by ID.
await env.LARRY.enqueueToChannel({ channelId: channels[0].channelId, content: `<@${studentId}> you're with <@${teacherId}>` });
```

**`syncRoles()`**

- A role is found by `id` when given, otherwise by exact name, otherwise created with no permissions. Two roles of the same name is an error for that entry; Larry won't guess.
- `color` (hex, e.g. `'#e67e22'`) sets the colour of a role Larry **creates**. A role that already exists keeps its colour, so one chosen in Discord is never overwritten.
- Everyone in `members` gets the role. With `exclusive: true`, **everyone else holding it loses it**, however they got it. With `exclusive: false` Larry only ever adds. The caller has to say which.
- Someone not in the server comes back in `notInServer`; call again later and they are picked up once they join.
- Larry refuses a role that carries moderation permissions (Administrator, Manage Roles, Kick, Ban and the like), one that belongs to a bot or integration, and @everyone. It manages labels, so that a caller with the binding can't make anyone a moderator.

**`setMemberRole()` and `enqueueMemberRole()`** change one role for one person: `{ userId, roleId, has }`. They are for a single change made on the spot, like a button press, where there is no repeating check behind it to put things right later. `setMemberRole()` does it now and throws if Discord refuses. `enqueueMemberRole()` checks the role, queues the change on `larry-messages` and returns; delivery retries rate limits and Discord outages the way queued messages do, and a change for someone not in the server is logged and dropped. Both sit behind the same guard as `syncRoles()`. To keep a whole role's membership in step, use `syncRoles()`, which needs no queue: it is called again on a schedule and only changes what is out of step.

**`syncChannels()`**

- A channel is found by `id`, otherwise by name under its category, otherwise created there. The name is lowercased and hyphenated the way Discord stores it (`Jo Rivera` → `#jo-rivera`).
- `category` is a name from `CHANNEL_CATEGORIES` (`name:categoryId`, like `SEND_CHANNELS`). Any other category is refused.
- A channel Larry **creates** is hidden from everyone but the roles in `visibleTo` and Larry itself. A channel it **finds** keeps its permissions exactly as they are.

**Both**

- `dryRun: true` makes no changes and returns what would happen. Run this first against a server where roles or channels were made by hand.
- One entry failing (returned with `error`) doesn't stop the others.
- Store the `roleId` and `channelId` that come back and pass them next time, so a rename in Discord doesn't make Larry create a second one.
- `rename: true` on an entry keeps the name in step: a role or channel found by its ID under a different name is renamed to the one asked for. Without it Larry never renames anything. Discord allows a channel only two renames in ten minutes.

**`deleteRoles()` and `deleteChannels()`** take IDs and remove them for good: a deleted role is gone for everyone who held it, and a deleted channel takes its messages with it. Neither can be undone. They sit behind the same fences as everything else here: no role with moderation permissions, no bot's role, and only text channels under a category in `CHANNEL_CATEGORIES`. One that is already gone comes back as `gone`, not an error. Both take `dryRun`.

`sendToChannel()` and `enqueueToChannel()` post by channel ID, and refuse any channel that isn't a text channel under a category in `CHANNEL_CATEGORIES`.

Setup, once:

1. Give Larry **Manage Channels** as well as Manage Roles, and move its role above every role it should manage. It can't hand out or remove a role that sits above its own.
2. In the Discord developer portal, switch on **Server Members Intent** for the bot. Without it Larry can't list who holds a role, so it still adds people but never removes anyone, and `canSeeMembers` comes back `false`.
3. Add the repository variables `ENV_GUILD_ID` (the server) and `ENV_CHANNEL_CATEGORIES` (e.g. `training:123456789`). Until `ENV_GUILD_ID` is set these methods refuse every call.

**Types for callers** are published to npm as [`@indy-center/indy-larry-worker`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@indy-center/indy-larry-worker), like `@indy-center/identity`: install it as a dependency and type the binding as `LARRY: LarryBinding`. It's types only, built from `worker/src/client/`; callers also need `@cloudflare/workers-types` (or `wrangler types`) for `Service` and `Rpc`. It's public so callers install it with no npm login, and it holds nothing that isn't already in this repo. To publish a change to `src/client/`: bump `version` in `worker/package.json` and merge. The `publish-types` job in `build-and-deploy.yml` publishes any version npm doesn't have yet, and does nothing when the version is unchanged. It uses npm's trusted publishing, so there is no token to keep: on npmjs.com the package's settings name this repository and the workflow file `build-and-deploy.yml`, with no environment. To publish by hand instead, run `npm publish` from `worker/` (needs publish rights on the `@indy-center` npm org; `prepublishOnly` builds `dist/`).

First time only, before the first deploy that includes the Worker:

1. Create the queues (needs `npx wrangler login` with the IndyCenter account): `cd worker && npx wrangler queues create larry-messages && npx wrangler queues create larry-messages-dlq && npx wrangler queues create role-sync && npx wrangler queues create role-sync-dlq && npx wrangler queues create teamspeak-results`
   `teamspeak-results` came later than the others: create it before the first deploy that carries teamspeak-bot's routes. The `TeamSpeakJobs` Durable Object needs nothing made by hand; its migration in `wrangler.jsonc` creates it on that deploy.
   The KV namespace `wrangler.jsonc` binds already exists (`indy-larry-worker`), and so must the Workers behind the `TRAINING` (`training-tools`) and `IDENTITY` (`id`) service bindings: a deploy fails if a bound service isn't deployed.
2. Add the `CLOUDFLARE_WORKERS_API_KEY` repository secret: a Cloudflare API token with **Workers Scripts:Edit** and **Queues:Edit**.
3. Add the `ENV_SEND_CHANNELS` repository variable.

To try it locally: `cd worker && npm install`, copy `.dev.vars.example` to `.dev.vars` with a test token and test channels, and run `npx wrangler dev`. A caller Worker run alongside it (`npx wrangler dev -c ../caller/wrangler.jsonc -c wrangler.jsonc`) can then use its `LARRY` binding.

## Role sync

The Worker replaces VATUSA's Discord bot and the legacy TeamSpeak bot: it works out each member's Discord roles and TeamSpeak server groups from VATUSA's lists, through one mapping file, `worker/src/mapping.ts`, and their Discord nickname. It runs **live** (`ROLE_SYNC_MODE`, Mode, below), changing members' mapped roles, groups and nicknames; switch it back to `dry-run` to log what it would change and change nothing in Discord or TeamSpeak.

- **Platforms**: the sync works out once per member who they are (their CID and its VATUSA record, from the run's lists or the per-user lookups) and which mapping entries they match, then applies that through each **platform adapter** they have an account on: Discord's, then TeamSpeak's (TeamSpeak, below). The shared part is `worker/src/role-sync.ts`: the cron, the queue consumer, the RPCs, the run's lists in KV and the job (`syncAccount`). The seam is the `Platform` interface in `worker/src/platforms/platform.ts`, and Discord's adapter is `worker/src/platforms/discord.ts`, which holds everything else in this section: listing the server, finding a Discord account's CID (identity's link, else VATUSA's Discord ID), the unresolved path and the shared-ID alert, the role-position guard, the group roles and the nickname. A job starts from one account: its adapter resolves it to a member, and every other adapter is asked for that member's account by CID and given the same entries. The member's identity record is read once per job and shared by the adapters. An account that resolves to no one loses its managed roles on its own platform only. **One job per member**: in a run, a member's job is the one of the first registered platform they have an account on, so a TeamSpeak run job whose member is in the Discord server stops (`Role sync: CID <cid> is synced by this run's discord job, not again from teamspeak`): Discord's job of the run applies both. `syncMembers` and the link writes queue one job per CID the same way. If another platform's account can't be looked up (identity down, say), that platform is skipped for the job with a warning and the job still succeeds. What each part of an adapter does:
  - `listAccounts(env, people, runId)`: for the cron, read what the run needs once (Discord: the server's roles, owner and larry's position, kept in the run's KV record as `server`) and list the accounts to queue (Discord: the server's members, as `run-member` jobs; TeamSpeak lists none here, but asks the bot for its accounts and queues their jobs when the answer comes). `freshRun(env)` reads the same for a job with no run of its own.
  - `owns(message)`, `jobOf(message)` and `accountOf(env, mode, message)`: which `role-sync` messages are its own, what each names (account, CID, run), and that account as it stands now (`live` reads a `run-member`'s roles again; `null`, logged, when it has gone).
  - `resolve(env, run, account, cid?)`: the account's member (`person`), a confirmed no one (`unresolved`: loses every managed role), or `left-alone` (Discord: a bot account, or an account another member has linked). It throws when a lookup fails.
  - `accountFor(env, cid, member)` and `current(env, ref)`: the account a CID syncs on, by the platform's rule (Discord: `getLinks`'s rule, below; TeamSpeak: identity's `teamspeak_uid`), with `member` the shared lookups (the newest run's record and the identity record, each read once), and that account as it stands now. `messageFor(cid, ref)` is the message that queues its job, `linkOf(ref)` what `getLinks` reports, and `unlink(env, cid)` clears the link.
  - `held(account)`, `idOf(entry)` and `skipped(run)`: the IDs the account holds, the role or group a mapping entry gives on the platform (Discord: `discordRoleId`; TeamSpeak: `teamspeakGroupId`), and the guard, IDs the run won't touch (Discord: mapped roles at or above larry's, @everyone, a bot's, or not on the server; TeamSpeak: none, since admin groups can't be in the mapping).
  - `apply(env, change)`: add and remove the entries the job worked out (`live`) or log them (`dry-run`), plus the platform's extras (Discord: the group roles and the nickname), and log the member's line.
- **Adding a platform**: write its adapter in `worker/src/platforms/` (the `Platform` interface), give the mapping's entries a field for it (`idOf` reads it), add its messages to `RoleSyncMessage` and its part of a run to `RunLists` in `worker/src/role-sync.ts`, and register it in `PLATFORMS` there, the one list of adapters. Its link goes in `Links` (`worker/src/client/api.ts`), keyed by its `name`. `worker/tests/role-sync-platforms.test.ts` shows the job with made-up adapters. `worker/tests/role-sync-teamspeak.test.ts` covers TeamSpeak's adapter, and `worker/tests/teamspeak-links.test.ts` its links.
- **The cron**, every 15 minutes (`triggers` in `wrangler.jsonc`), fetches VATUSA's public lists (no key): `roster/both` for ZID, ZME, ZDC, ZAU, ZOB, ZTL and ZKC, `integration/staff`, and `user/roles/ZHQ/ACE`. It keeps them in the `KV` namespace (`indy-larry-worker`) for that run and queues one job per member of the server on `role-sync`, and asks teamspeak-bot for TeamSpeak's accounts, whose jobs follow when it answers (TeamSpeak, below).
- **A join** syncs that member within a minute, not at the next cron. The bot on the VPS sends `POST https://indy-larry.indy-center.workers.dev/bot/events` with `{ "type": "sync-member", "discordId": "<id>" }` and `Authorization: Bearer <BOT_EVENTS_SECRET>` on `guildMemberAdd`, and the Worker queues a `sync-member` job on `role-sync`. That job syncs the member the way `syncMembers(cids)` does (below): the roles they hold now, and the newest run's lists while they are kept, else the per-user lookups. If the member has already left, it's dropped and logged. A send the bot can't make (network error, non-2xx, or no answer in 10 seconds) is logged and dropped; the next cron catches them.
- **The route** (`worker/src/bot-events.ts`, on the `workers_dev` address in `wrangler.jsonc`; preview URLs are off, with `preview_urls: false`): `POST /bot/events` answers `202` once queued, `401` for a missing or wrong secret (compared in constant time, `worker/src/auth.ts`), and `400` for a body that isn't a known type; `sync-member` is the only one. Every other path or method gets `404`, apart from teamspeak-bot's routes (below). A new gateway event becomes a new type here, not a new route. The secret is the `ENV_BOT_EVENTS_SECRET` setting, which the bot reads from its `.env` and the deploy sets on the Worker as `BOT_EVENTS_SECRET`; with it unset the route refuses everything.
- **Group roles**: each member's job also gives and takes the roles of the groups apps serve ([Group sync](#group-sync), below). A training outage skips only training's groups; VATUSA's roles sync as usual.
- **Discord links**: identity is a plain fetch handler, so larry calls its `/api/users` routes through the `IDENTITY` service binding (the `id` Worker) with a client-credentials token (`POST /oauth/token`, kept until it is about to expire). The client is registered in identity with the `users:read` and `users:write` scopes; its ID is the `IDENTITY_CLIENT_ID` var (repo variable `ENV_IDENTITY_CLIENT_ID`) and its secret the `IDENTITY_CLIENT_SECRET` Worker secret (repo secret `ENV_IDENTITY_CLIENT_SECRET`), both set by the deploy; for `wrangler dev` put them in `.dev.vars`. All of it goes through `worker/src/identity.ts`. Identity has no "list every user" route, so each member's job asks for the user by Discord ID (`GET /api/users?discord_id=`), and a member found only through VATUSA is checked with `GET /api/users/{cid}`. A member whose Discord ID is some identity user's `discord_id` takes that CID, whatever VATUSA says: a CID's VATUSA Discord ID is never used for `syncMembers`, `postToGroup`'s mentions, `unlink`, `getLinks` or a queued `sync-cid` job once another member has linked that account. The VATUSA account of a CID that has linked a different one gets no roles (`unresolved (their CID is linked to another Discord account)`). If identity can't be reached the job throws and is retried like any other.
- **The consumer** finds each member's CID (their identity link, else the one whose VATUSA Discord ID is theirs) and logs one line: `Role sync (dry run): <Discord ID> CID <cid>: add <roles>; remove <roles>; nickname "<name>"`, roles by mapping name (`(live)` in place of `(dry run)` once it changes them; the nickname part only when it would change, or `nickname left alone (<why>)`). A member on none of the lists is looked up in VATUSA by Discord ID (`/v2/user/{discordId}?d`, public), and a found record is evaluated like a list member; a 404 leaves them `unresolved`, and they lose their managed roles (below). Those lookups are cached in KV for 24 hours, found or not, and go out at most 20 a second. A member whose CID is known but whom VATUSA has no record of takes their rating from VATSIM (`api.vatsim.net/v2/members/{cid}`, public, cached 24 hours). A job that throws is retried, then parked in `role-sync-dlq`.
- **The mapping** is in VATUSA's criteria and conditions shape, seeded from VATUSA's `config/zid.yaml` with the same names and Discord role IDs. An entry matches when any of its criteria does, and a criterion when all of its conditions do. The conditions are `rating`, `home`, `home_or_visit` (on that facility's roster), `facility_role` (`FAC:ROLE`), `role` (at any facility), `division_staff` (a role starting `US` with a digit, so not `USWT`) and `all`. Nobody rated below 1 matches anything, so `Member` (`all`) goes to every known CID rated 1 or above. Training credentials reach Discord as groups, not through the mapping. Only roles the mapping names are ever added or removed; a hand-given role is left alone. Change it by pull request: a wrong entry hands its role to everyone it matches, and the sync may hand out roles with moderation permissions.
- **Which roles it may change**: any mapped role below larry's highest role in Discord's role list. Unlike `syncRoles()` and `setMemberRole()`, the sync doesn't refuse roles with moderation permissions; it refuses only @everyone and a bot's or integration's role. Discord's role order is the guard: keep a role the sync must never touch above larry's. Once per run the cron reads the server's roles, and a mapped role at or above larry's highest (or @everyone, or a bot's) is skipped for that run with one log line, `Role sync: skipping mapped role <name> this run: <why>`. A mapped role that isn't on the server at all is skipped the same way, for every member and for resolved and unresolved ones alike (never added, never removed), with one log line per role per run, `Role sync: mapped role <name> (<role ID>) isn't on the server; skipping it.`; a member's other roles still sync. A join (`sync-member`) and a job by CID (`sync-cid`) read the server's roles the same way. It fails nothing.
- **Unresolved members lose their managed roles**, as with VATUSA's bot, which removes every role in its config from a member its lookup answers 404 for. Without it, once VATUSA's bot is retired, an account that stops resolving would keep its roles for good. Unresolved is a confirmed "no one": VATUSA has no record of them (404) and identity has no link to them, or neither VATUSA nor VATSIM knows the CID they linked, or their Discord ID is on VATUSA for more than one CID, or their CID has linked a different Discord account (so an account unlinked in identity, and not anyone's VATUSA Discord ID, loses its roles at the next run). The log line reads `Role sync (dry run): <Discord ID> unresolved[ (<why>)]: remove <roles>`; `live` removes them. Only mapped roles below larry's go (Which roles it may change, above); their nickname is left alone. A lookup that fails (VATUSA, VATSIM or identity answering anything but a 404, or not answering) removes nothing: the job throws and is retried. Discord bot accounts are left alone, roles and all (`<Discord ID> is a bot account: left alone`). An account another member has linked in identity, met in a `sync-cid` job for someone else, keeps its roles (`unresolved (this Discord account is linked to another member): no changes`): its own job syncs it.
- **Nicknames** (`worker/src/nickname-rule.ts`), for a member with a known CID rated 1 or above, the first that applies: identity's `name` (their preferred name, else their VATSIM first and last name) whenever identity knows them, without VATUSA's name-privacy flag; else VATUSA's "First Last", or "First CID" when that flag is on. A name over 32 characters is shortened as VATUSA's bot does (First Last, then First L, then First, then the first name cut short). Anyone else keeps their nickname. The names come from the identity user the job already looks up for the member's link; a job by CID (`sync-cid`) asks identity for them, and if identity can't be reached it logs that and uses VATUSA's name. larry leaves only the server's owner, itself and anyone holding a role at or above its own alone, since Discord refuses those; administrators below its role get synced nicknames like everyone else. It needs **Manage Nicknames**.
- **Safety**: if any list can't be fetched, or a roster or the staff list comes back empty, the run queues nothing and larry posts "Role sync skipped: couldn't fetch … from VATUSA." to `tech-team-alerts` (a `SEND_CHANNELS` name, so that channel must be listed there). It posts once per outage; the next good run clears it.
- **A shared Discord ID**: when VATUSA's lists give one Discord ID to two or more CIDs, that member is `unresolved` and loses their managed roles (above). The job logs one line naming the Discord ID and every CID, and larry posts "Role sync: Discord account <discordId> is on VATUSA for more than one member (CIDs <cid>, <cid>). It gets no roles until one of them links it on their profile." to `tech-team-alerts`, in `dry-run` and `live` alike. It posts once per Discord ID (kept in KV); the next run that no longer finds the ID shared clears it, so a recurrence posts again. A member who has linked that account in identity takes the linked CID: no log, no alert.
- **The link RPCs** (types in `worker/src/client/api.ts`), for the community website's profile and admin buttons. `linkDiscord(cid, discordId, accessToken?)` writes the link and queues that member's sync; the caller has already proven the account. It throws "That Discord account is linked to another member." if another identity user holds it, and "Sign in to flyindycenter.com once before linking." if identity has no user for the CID, before anything else happens. **Joining**: when the caller also passes the member's one-time Discord access token (from a sign-in that asked for `identify guilds.join`), larry adds them to the server after writing the link: `PUT /guilds/{GUILD_ID}/members/{discordId}` with `{ access_token }`, as the "Indy Center" app's bot (`DISCORD_JOIN_BOT_TOKEN`). Discord takes that call only with the bot token of the app that issued the member's token, so larry's own bot can't make it; the join bot is in the server with only **Create Instant Invite**. It answers `{ joined: true }` when Discord added them (201) and `{ joined: false }` when they were already in it (204), without a token, or while `DISCORD_JOIN_BOT_TOKEN` is unset (which logs a warning). The join, the link and the queued sync happen in both modes: the member asked to join, and `ROLE_SYNC_MODE` (Mode, below) governs only the sync's role, group and nickname changes. The token is sent to Discord for that one call and never kept or logged. If the join fails (banned, the join bot missing from the server or its permission, Discord down, a bad token), the link stays and the sync is still queued, Discord's answer is logged, and it throws "Your Discord account is linked, but we couldn't add you to the Indy Center Discord. Ask a staff member for an invite." A member who joins is also synced by the join path above, which is harmless. `unlink(cid, 'discord')` clears it and queues the sync of the Discord ID VATUSA holds, if any. `confirmTsLink(cid, token)` and `unlink(cid, 'teamspeak')` are TeamSpeak's (TeamSpeak links, below). `getLinks(cid)` returns `{ discord: { id, source: 'linked' | 'vatusa' } | null, teamspeak: { uid } | null }`, `teamspeak` being the UID in identity's `teamspeak_uid`.
- **`syncMembers(cids)`** syncs up to 100 members now, for an app that has just changed something about them (a credential granted, a student assigned) and for the profile buttons (`syncMembers([cid])`). It reads every group source again ([Group sync](#group-sync)), then queues each CID's one sync (a `sync-cid` message on `role-sync`, or `ts-sync-cid` for a member with a TeamSpeak link and no Discord account; the consumer reads their roles from the server and uses the newest run's lists while they are kept, else the per-user lookups, and applies to both platforms). It answers one entry per CID: `{ cid, queued: true }`, or `{ cid, queued: false, reason }` when no Discord account or TeamSpeak identity is known for the CID, or when the member was queued in the last 2 minutes. A CID's account is found by the sync's rule: their identity link, else their VATUSA Discord ID unless another member has linked it or the lists give it to more than one CID. It follows `ROLE_SYNC_MODE`. It replaces 1.2.0's `syncMember(cid)`.
- **TeamSpeak jobs**: larry holds the changes for TeamSpeak itself, and teamspeak-bot (in `Indy-Center/teamspeak-server`'s `bot/`) pulls and applies them over ServerQuery. Inside the Worker, `enqueueTsGroups(env, uid, add, remove)` (`worker/src/teamspeak-jobs.ts`; not an RPC method) adds a `set-groups` job, `{ jobId, type: 'set-groups', uid, add, remove }` with server group IDs, and returns its ID; the result comes back later. The jobs live in the `TeamSpeakJobs` Durable Object (SQLite storage, one instance by a fixed name, `TEAMSPEAK_JOBS` in `wrangler.jsonc`). A pull hands out the oldest jobs that aren't leased, each leased for 60 seconds; one not acked by then can be pulled again. An ack deletes a job. A retry, or a lease running out, counts one try, and the 5th moves the job to the object's `dead_jobs` table with one error line, `TeamSpeak job <jobId> moved to the dead-letter table after 5 tries (last: <why>): <job>`. The job and result shapes are in `worker/src/teamspeak-shape.ts`; teamspeak-bot keeps a copy of that file (no shared package), so a change is made in both repos. Its tests run the object's class directly over a real SQLite database in memory (Node's `node:sqlite`) standing in for its storage (`fakeDurableObjects` in `worker/tests/fake-cloudflare.ts`).
- **teamspeak-bot's routes** (`worker/src/teamspeak-routes.ts`, on the same address and handler as `/bot/events`), all `POST` with `Authorization: Bearer <TEAMSPEAK_BOT_SECRET>`: `/teamspeak/jobs/pull` takes `{ "max": n }` (a whole number from 1; over 10 gives 10) and answers `200 { "jobs": [...] }`; `/teamspeak/jobs/ack` takes `{ "acks": [jobId], "retries": [jobId] }` and answers `204` (IDs it no longer holds are ignored); `/teamspeak/results` takes one result or a list, puts each on the `teamspeak-results` queue and answers `202`. The results are `{ type: 'applied', jobId }`, `{ type: 'failed', jobId, error? }`, `{ type: 'heartbeat', at }`, `{ type: 'reconnected', at }`, `{ type: 'accounts', holders: [{ uid, groups }], connected: [uid] }` (the answer to `report-accounts`; TeamSpeak, below) and `{ type: 'client-connected', uid, clientId, nickname }` (a client entering the server, query clients excluded; `clientId` a whole number; TeamSpeak links, below), `at` an ISO 8601 time; a body holding anything else gets `400` and queues nothing, as does a pull or ack body that isn't the shape above. Each route takes only teamspeak-bot's secret, and `/bot/events` only larry's bot's: the other bot's secret, a wrong one or none gets `401`. The secret is the `ENV_TEAMSPEAK_BOT_SECRET` setting, which the deploy sets on the Worker as `TEAMSPEAK_BOT_SECRET` and teamspeak-server's settings hold as teamspeak-bot's `LARRY_SECRET`; with it unset the routes refuse everything. The `teamspeak-results` consumer logs each result: `TeamSpeak job <jobId> applied`, `TeamSpeak job <jobId> failed: <error>` (a warning), `teamspeak-bot heartbeat at <at>`, `teamspeak-bot reconnected to ServerQuery at <at>`, hands `accounts` to TeamSpeak's adapter and `client-connected` to the TeamSpeak links; a result that throws there is retried on its own.
- **TeamSpeak** (`worker/src/platforms/teamspeak.ts`), the second platform: a TeamSpeak identity's unique ID (UID) is its account, and the mapping's `teamspeakGroupId` the server group (sgid) an entry gives. The IDs are the legacy TeamSpeak bot's, its roles table matched by name to the server's groups. The cron queues one `report-accounts` job, `{ jobId, type: 'report-accounts', groups }`, with every group the mapping names; teamspeak-bot answers with one `accounts` result: `holders`, every identity in the client database holding one of those groups with all its server groups, and `connected`, the UIDs connected now (query clients excluded). larry keeps the holders' groups in KV for an hour (`role-sync:teamspeak:accounts`) and queues one `ts-run-account` job per UID named, logging `Role sync run <runId>: teamspeak-bot reported <n> identities holding a managed group and <n> connected; queued <n>`. An answer no run is waiting for (`role-sync:teamspeak:asked`) is logged and dropped. If the bot hasn't answered by the next cron, that run's TeamSpeak part is skipped: `Role sync: TeamSpeak's part of run <runId> skipped: teamspeak-bot didn't answer report-accounts before the next run` (a warning); so is a run whose `report-accounts` can't be queued, and Discord's part goes on either way. Each job resolves its UID with identity's `GET /api/users?teamspeak_uid=` (exact match); the member is the CID it finds, with VATUSA's record (the run's lists, else the per-user lookup) or, failing that, VATSIM's rating. A member's current groups are the latest kept answer's (a UID it doesn't name holds no managed group; with no answer kept, TeamSpeak waits for the next run). The change goes out as one `set-groups` job, in `live` only and only when something differs, and only groups the mapping names are ever in it. A UID no member has linked, or whose member neither VATUSA nor VATSIM knows, is unresolved and loses every managed group, as does a member rated below 1; a lookup that fails retries the job. The log line reads `Role sync (live): TeamSpeak <uid> CID <cid>: add <entries>; remove <entries>` or `Role sync (live): TeamSpeak <uid> unresolved: remove <entries>`. **Admin groups**: 6 Server Admin, 8 Guest, 27 teamspeak-bot and the query groups 1 and 2 are hard-coded (`ADMIN_GROUPS` in `worker/src/mapping.ts`); a mapping entry naming one fails the mapping's load with `Mapping entry <name>: TeamSpeak group <id> is an admin group`.
- **TeamSpeak links** (`worker/src/platforms/teamspeak-links.ts`): a member links a TeamSpeak identity only by opening the link teamspeak-bot sends it privately when it connects unlinked; no route or RPC lists connected clients or sends a link on request. On `client-connected`, larry looks the UID up in identity (`GET /api/users?teamspeak_uid=`). A UID a member holds is logged (`TeamSpeak <uid> connected: linked to CID <cid>`), sent no link, and its member is synced (On connect, below). For a UID no member holds, larry makes a token (32 random bytes, URL-safe base64), keeps `teamspeak-link:token:<token>` → UID and `teamspeak-link:sent:<uid>` → when it was sent in KV for 15 minutes, and queues a `send-text` job, `{ jobId, type: 'send-text', clientId, text }`, that teamspeak-bot sends as a private message to that client: "Link this TeamSpeak identity to your Indy Center account: https://flyindycenter.com/profile?ts=<token> (works once, for 15 minutes)". While that link is unused and under 15 minutes old, a reconnect sends nothing; after it expires or is used, the next connect sends a fresh one. The token is never logged. If identity can't be reached the result is retried. `confirmTsLink(cid, token)` (an RPC, for the community website's `/profile?ts=`) refuses, before anything else happens, a token KV no longer holds (unknown, expired or used: "This link has expired or was already used. Reconnect to TeamSpeak and we'll send you a new one."), a UID another member holds ("That TeamSpeak identity is linked to another member.") and a CID identity has no user for ("Sign in to flyindycenter.com once before linking."). Otherwise it deletes the token, takes every managed group from the member's old UID if they had a different one (a `set-groups` job removing every group the mapping names), writes `teamspeak_uid` (`PATCH /api/users/{cid}`; a 409 is the "another member" refusal) and queues the member's sync (`ts-sync-cid`, which applies to Discord too). `unlink(cid, 'teamspeak')` queues the same removal for the member's UID, then clears `teamspeak_uid` and queues the sync of any account they still have. The link writes happen in both modes, as Discord's do; the `send-text` and `set-groups` jobs are TeamSpeak writes and follow Mode (below).
- **Seeding TeamSpeak links at the cutover** (`worker/scripts/seed-ts-links.ts`, `worker/src/teamspeak-seed.ts`): a one-off that sends the legacy bot's links (the `members` table of its `database.sqlite`: Discord ID, TeamSpeak unique ID) to larry, so most members needn't link again. It is one more route on the same handler, `POST /teamspeak/seed` with `{ rows: [{ discordId, uid }], dryRun }` (at most 50 rows), behind a secret of its own, `TEAMSPEAK_SEED_SECRET`; like the other routes it takes only its own secret (none, a wrong one or another bot's gets `401`), and its secret is refused on theirs. With it unset the route refuses everything, which is its normal state. Each row's Discord ID is matched to a CID (identity's `GET /api/users?discord_id=`, else VATUSA's lists, read once per request), then the UID is written with confirmTsLink's checks and write (identity's `teamspeak_uid`, the member's sync queued), in both modes as the other link writes are. The answer gives each row one outcome: *written*; *already linked* (the member already has a UID, which is left alone); *UID on another member* (skipped); *extra UID* (the member's second and later rows; the first is written); *no CID* (dropped; they get the link message on their next connect); *never signed in* (the CID is unknown to identity; dropped). With `dryRun: true` the same outcomes are worked out and nothing is written or queued. A request that fails part way (identity or VATUSA down) answers `502` or `503` with the rows done so far; running it again is safe, since a written row is *already linked* the second time. **To run it at cutover**, after teamspeak-bot is up and the legacy bot is stopped (Rollout step 4): (1) make a secret (`openssl rand -base64 32`), set it as the `ENV_TEAMSPEAK_SEED_SECRET` repo secret and deploy (the deploy skips it while that is unset); (2) in `worker/`, with Node 22.18 or later, dry run first: `LARRY_URL=https://indy-larry.indy-center.workers.dev TEAMSPEAK_SEED_SECRET=<secret> npm run seed-ts-links -- <path to database.sqlite> --dry-run`, and read the report; (3) run it again without `--dry-run`; (4) remove the secret: delete the repo secret `ENV_TEAMSPEAK_SEED_SECRET` and the Worker's with `npx wrangler secret delete TEAMSPEAK_SEED_SECRET` (the deploy never removes one), after which the route refuses everything. The script sends a Discord ID's rows together, in batches of at most 50 rows, and prints the report: a count per outcome, and every row that wasn't written with its Discord ID and UID. It finds the table's columns by name (a Discord ID and a `teamspeakID`, any case or underscores) and stops with the columns it found when it can't.
- **On connect, on reconnect, and the quiet alert** (`worker/src/platforms/teamspeak-links.ts`, `syncOnConnect` in `worker/src/role-sync.ts`, `worker/src/teamspeak-quiet.ts`): a `client-connected` for a UID a member holds queues that member's one job (as `syncMembers` does), unless they were synced in the last 5 minutes: an earlier connect's job (`role-sync:connect:<cid>` in KV, 5 minutes) or a `syncMembers` one (`role-sync:member:<cid>`, 2 minutes) stands, logged as `Role sync: CID <cid> connected to TeamSpeak; synced in the last 5 minutes, so no new sync`. A `reconnected` result (teamspeak-bot's ServerQuery connection is back after TeamSpeak restarted) runs a full sync, the cron's `startRoleSync`: fresh lists, a new `report-accounts`, every member's job. larry keeps the time of the last result of any kind from teamspeak-bot, heartbeats included (`teamspeak-bot:last-result` in KV). The cron checks it before its run: with none for 30 minutes it posts "teamspeak-bot hasn't reported in 30 minutes. TeamSpeak groups aren't syncing." to `tech-team-alerts` once (`teamspeak-bot:quiet` in KV), and the next result posts "teamspeak-bot is back." and clears it, so a later silence posts again. If larry has never kept a time (the bot hasn't reported yet), the cron posts nothing. Both alerts post in `dry-run` and `live`, and need `tech-team-alerts` in `SEND_CHANNELS`, as the other alerts do.
- **Mode**: the `ROLE_SYNC_MODE` var in `wrangler.jsonc`, for both platforms. `dry-run` (the default) logs the roles, groups and nickname it would change and changes nothing: it queues no `set-groups` or `send-text` job (it still asks teamspeak-bot for its accounts, which changes nothing). The one exception is Link Discord's join, which happens in both modes (the link RPCs, above). So in `dry-run` an unlinked identity that connects is sent no link (`Link TeamSpeak (dry run): would send TeamSpeak <uid> a link`), and an unlink or relink logs `Link TeamSpeak (dry run): would remove every managed group from TeamSpeak <uid>` and still writes the link. `live` changes them: each member's job reads their roles and nickname from the server again, then adds and removes their mapped roles, sets their nickname and queues their `set-groups`. A Discord failure fails that member's job, so the queue retries it and then parks it in `role-sync-dlq`; the other members' jobs carry on. To switch, change `"ROLE_SYNC_MODE": "dry-run"` to `"live"` in `worker/wrangler.jsonc` and merge (the deploy sets it), or back to `dry-run` to stop changing Discord and TeamSpeak; no code changes. Any other value makes the cron and the consumer refuse to run.

It needs the Server Members intent to list the server; without it the cron fails and queues nothing. The bot asks for the same intent (Guild Members) when `WORKER_URL` and `BOT_EVENTS_SECRET` are set, to hear joins; with either blank the join sync is off. To run the cron locally: `npx wrangler dev --test-scheduled`, then open `http://localhost:8787/__scheduled`.

## Group sync

Apps serve **groups**, and larry turns each into a Discord role, plus a private channel for a group with a room, and keeps them in step; the role sync puts the roles on each member's account. larry knows nothing about why a group exists: the app owns which groups there are, who is in them and what they're called. Groups are strict: a group's members are the whole truth, and anyone else loses its role. The training app (`training-tools`, through the `TRAINING` binding) is the only source today. The code is `worker/src/groups.ts`.

- **The contract**: a source implements `GroupSource` from `@indy-center/indy-larry-worker` (1.3.0): `getGroups(): Promise<GroupsAnswer>`, where `GroupsAnswer` is `{ groups: { key, name, members, room?, hold? }[] }`. `key` is stable within the source (renaming one makes a new group and retires the old one after the grace period below); `name` is the role's name; `members` are VATSIM CIDs as digit strings, no duplicates; `room` is `{ name, fallbackName }`; `hold: true` means keep the group exactly as it is: not managed, renamed or deleted, and not made if larry has nothing for it yet. Members are CIDs, never Discord accounts: larry places each CID's account the way the role sync does, and an account it can't place gets no group roles.
- **Adding a source**: a service binding in `wrangler.jsonc` to the app's entrypoint, its name in `SourceBinding` and on `GroupSourcesEnv`, a mode setting (below), and an entry in `worker/src/group-sources.ts`: `source` (its name, for KV, logs, alerts and the RPCs), `binding`, `roomCategory` (a `CHANNEL_CATEGORIES` name its rooms go under), `roomViewers` (role IDs that also see every room larry creates; for training, the training admins) and `modeSetting`. The tests check the file.
- **Mode**, per source: its setting (`GROUP_SYNC_TRAINING`, from the `ENV_GROUP_SYNC_TRAINING` repo variable) is `off` (or unset, or anything else), `dry-run` or `live`. `off` reads nothing and manages none of its roles. `dry-run` works out, logs and reports every change and makes none, except storing the IDs of roles and channels it finds; members' log lines then read `<source> groups (dry run): add …; remove …`. `ROLE_SYNC_MODE` `dry-run` makes nothing for any group either. Switch by changing the repo variable and running the deploy.
- **Each cron run**, before the role sync lists the server, reads each source that isn't `off` and checks its answer. An answer whose call fails, that is malformed (a group with no key, a repeated key, a bad name or member, a room without both names) or that trips the drop guard is **refused**: that source's roles aren't managed that run, its groups are left exactly as they are, and larry posts once per outage to `tech-team-alerts`. VATUSA's roles sync as usual. An accepted answer becomes the source's latest copy, kept an hour; a refused one drops the copy, so that run's member jobs leave the source's roles alone until a read is accepted again. A run VATUSA's outage skips reads no source either.
- **Making**: for each served group, its role is found by stored ID, else by exact name, else created with no permissions at the bottom of the list, and renamed if its name drifts. A group with a room gets a text channel under `roomCategory`, found the same way (stored ID, then name in the category, then created), visible only to the group's role, `roomViewers` and larry, and renamed to match; a channel larry finds keeps its permissions. Two rooms whose names are the same in Discord both use their `fallbackName`. At most 25 roles and channels are created a run; the rest wait for the next run. Each group's record is KV `group:<source>:<key>` = `{ roleId, channelId, missingSince }`.
- **Guards**: a group role that is also in `mapping.ts` is ignored, with one log line a run. A role carrying moderation permissions is refused, the same rule as `syncRoles()`: this matters most when a role is first matched by name, since a clash would otherwise hand a moderator role to a group and take it from everyone else. A role at or above larry's highest, @everyone or a bot's role is skipped and logged. A refused role is `failed` in the status, alerts, and nothing is created in its place.
- **Going**: a stored group the source no longer serves is unmanaged at once (its role and room left exactly as they are) and alerts once. Served again, it is managed again. The time it stopped is kept in `dry-run` too, so the status can say it would be deleted. 48 hours after it stopped being served, its channel is deleted, then its role; the record goes only once Discord confirms each, and a failed deletion alerts and is tried again next run.
- **Applying**: every member job (cron, join, link, `syncMembers`) manages the roles of the groups in use in each source's latest copy, as it stands when the job runs: a resolved member gets the roles of the groups listing their CID and loses the others; an unresolved account loses them all, as with mapped roles. A job with no copy of a source's groups (expired, or every read refused) leaves that source's roles alone.
- **The drop guard**: if the distinct CIDs across a source's groups fall by more than half, and by more than 10, against its last accepted answer, the answer is refused. If the drop is real, reset the last answer and the next run takes it: `cd worker && npx wrangler kv key delete --binding KV --remote group-sync:<source>:last` (e.g. `group-sync:training:last`).
- **`syncMembers(cids)`** ([Role sync](#role-sync)) reads every source again first, with the same checks, so an app's change lands within a minute; a refused read leaves the jobs on the latest accepted copy. It makes no roles or channels: a new group waits for the next cron run.
- **`postToGroup({ source, key, notice, mention? })`** posts a notice in a group's room: `notice` is `{ title, body, fields?: { name, value }[] }`, plain text that larry escapes, except that each `http://` or `https://` URL, up to the next whitespace, is kept as it is so its link works (Discord's limits are measured on the escaped text), and `mention` is a list of CIDs. larry reads the source again, syncs each mentioned member's roles now (so a student just assigned can see the room), then queues the post mentioning their accounts; a CID with no account is left unmentioned. It answers `'posted'` (queued), `'no-room'` (the group has no room, or none made yet) or `'not-live'` (the source's mode isn't `live`, or `ROLE_SYNC_MODE` is `dry-run`, so the mentioned members couldn't be given the room).
- **`getGroupStatus(source)`** answers what the last cron run did: `{ mode, at, refused?, groups: { key, name, role, room?, error? }[] }`, each state one of `found`, `created`, `renamed`, `held`, `missing`, `deleted` or `failed`. In `dry-run` the states say what would happen; `refused` says why that run's answer was refused.
- **Alerts** go to `tech-team-alerts` once per outage: a refused answer, a role or channel that can't be made, renamed or deleted, and a group that stops being served.

## Project layout

- `src/bot.js`: logs in, polls every `POLL_SECONDS`, and posts, edits and deletes embeds.
- `src/botEvents.js`: tells larry's Worker when someone joins the server (the join sync).
- `src/ironmic.js`: Iron Mic positions, vNAS Stats totals and the leaderboard embed. `src/ironmicCommand.js`: the `/ironmic` command.
- `src/feed.js`: fetches the [vNAS controller feed](https://docs.virtualnas.net/data-admin/controller-feed/), the vNAS ARTCC data (facility and position list) and [VATSIM ATC bookings](https://atc-bookings.vatsim.net/), groups controllers by facility and tracks activation times.
- `src/status.js`: decides each facility's status and parses "Online until" from controller info.
- `src/embeds.js`: the embed layout.
- `src/traffic.js`: counts aircraft on each position's frequency from the transceivers feed, and decides when a position is busy enough to alert.
- `src/congestion.js`: airspace congestion: boundaries, airborne and entering counts, who's working each area, and the alert.
- `src/names.js`: CMS position lookup following Centerline's rules, and the airport for a position.
- `src/errorReport.js`: reports errors, crashes and up/down to `#larry-errors` (see [Error reports](#error-reports)).
- `src/stateFile.js`: saves the JSON state files so a crash never leaves one half-written.
- `src/notify.js`: the notification panels: button and menu handling, role timers and `notify.json`.
- `src/panels.js`: the panel, form and request alert layouts.
- `test/`: unit tests. `test/backup/` is the backup round trip (see [Backups](#backups)).
- `worker/`: the send Worker (TypeScript, its own `package.json`). `src/index.ts` is the RPC entrypoint, cron, queue consumer and fetch handler, `src/bot-events.ts` the bots' routes (with `src/auth.ts`, the secret check, and `src/teamspeak-routes.ts`, teamspeak-bot's routes and the `teamspeak-results` consumer), `src/teamspeak-jobs.ts` the TeamSpeak jobs Durable Object and `enqueueTsGroups()` (with `src/teamspeak-shape.ts`, the jobs' and results' shapes), `src/send.ts` checks and sends messages, `src/errors.ts` reports errors to `#larry-errors`, `src/role-sync.ts` is the role sync's shared part and `syncMembers()`, with its platform adapters in `src/platforms/` (`platform.ts` the interface, `discord.ts` Discord's adapter and `linkDiscord()`, `teamspeak.ts` TeamSpeak's adapter and its `accounts` handling) (with `src/mapping.ts`, `src/criteria.ts`, `src/vatusa.ts`, `src/lookups.ts` for the per-user lookups, `src/identity.ts` for identity's Discord and TeamSpeak links and names, `src/nickname-rule.ts` for choosing a nickname and `src/nicknames.ts` for setting one), `src/groups.ts` is the group sync (with `src/group-sources.ts`, its sources, and `src/group-rpc.ts`, `postToGroup()`), `src/alerts.ts` posts to `tech-team-alerts`, `src/channels.ts` reads `SEND_CHANNELS`, `src/client/` holds the types callers import, `src/teamspeak-seed.ts` the cutover's seed route, `scripts/check-channels.ts` is the deploy's `SEND_CHANNELS` check, `scripts/seed-ts-links.ts` the cutover's seed script, and `tests/` its unit tests (`npm test` in `worker/`).
- `Dockerfile`: the image, published as `ghcr.io/indy-center/vnas-discord-bot`.
- `deploy/docker-compose.yml`: what's deployed to `/home/deploy/apps/indy-larry/` on the VPS. Keeps `state.json` in the `bot-state` volume.
- `deploy/.env.example`: every setting the bot reads, with its default. Names only, no values.
- `deploy/backup.sh`, `deploy/restore.sh`: back up and restore the `bot-state` volume (see [Backups](#backups)).
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml`: tests, compose validation, an image build and the backup round trip, plus the Worker's typecheck, tests and bundle, on every pull request.
- `.github/workflows/build-and-deploy.yml`: runs CI, pushes the image to GHCR, then rsyncs `deploy/` to the VPS and runs `docker compose up -d` over SSH. Alongside that it deploys the Worker to Cloudflare.

## Deployment

The bot runs on the Vanderbilt VPS, following the [VPS apps pattern](https://tech.flyindycenter.com/patterns/vps-apps/). It only makes outbound calls (Discord, vNAS, VATSIM, the Indy Center CMS, GitHub for airspace boundaries), so it has no Traefik labels, doesn't join `traefik-shared` and publishes no ports.

`build-and-deploy.yml` runs on every push to `main`, and by hand from **Actions → Build and Deploy → Run workflow**. It calls `ci.yml` and only deploys if it passes. It builds the image and pushes it to `ghcr.io/indy-center/vnas-discord-bot`, tagged `latest` and with the commit SHA. It then rsyncs `deploy/` to `/home/deploy/apps/indy-larry/`, writes `.env` there from the `ENV_*` secrets and variables, pulls and runs `docker compose up -d`, and fails if anything is restarting 15 seconds later.

The app directory and compose project are named after the repository, so renaming the repo deploys a second copy next to the old one, with its own empty volume. Two copies with the same token repost the panels and double up status embeds. After a rename, stop the old one on the VPS: `cd ~/apps/<old name> && docker compose down`.

Secrets:

- **Deploy credentials**: the `VANDERBILT_HOST` and `VANDERBILT_DEPLOY_USER` organization variables and the `VANDERBILT_DEPLOY_SSH_KEY` and `VANDERBILT_KNOWN_HOSTS` organization secrets. An org admin adds this repository to the repository access of all four.
- **Runtime settings**: one repository secret or variable per setting in [`deploy/.env.example`](deploy/.env.example), named `ENV_<NAME>`, plus a matching line in the **Write .env** step of `.github/workflows/build-and-deploy.yml`. Either kind works (a secret wins if both exist), except `ENV_DISCORD_TOKEN`, which must be a secret. Every deploy writes the settings that are set to `/home/deploy/apps/indy-larry/.env` as `NAME='value'`, readable only by `deploy`; unset ones are left out so the defaults apply. A value can't contain a single quote or a newline; the deploy fails with the setting's name before anything reaches the VPS.

### First deploy

1. An org admin adds this repository to the `VANDERBILT_*` secrets.
2. Add the `ENV_*` secret and variables from the table above.
3. Stop any other copy of the bot.
4. Run **Build and Deploy**. The first run pushes the image, then fails at the pull, because new GHCR packages start private.
5. Make the package public: **Indy-Center → Packages → vnas-discord-bot → Package settings → Change visibility → Public**.
6. Run **Build and Deploy** again, then check the run's log, or on the VPS:

   ```bash
   cd /home/deploy/apps/indy-larry && docker compose ps && docker compose logs --tail 50
   ```

## Backups

The VPS's backup runner backs the bot up nightly, as it does every app (see [Backups in docker-infrastructure](https://github.com/Indy-Center/docker-infrastructure#backups)). A backup holds everything in the `bot-state` volume: `state.json`, `notify.json`, `ironmic.json`, `congestion.json` and `uptime.json`. After a restore the bot edits the same status messages and panels, keeps its role timers, request alerts and congestion alert memory, and carries on any running Iron Mic.

- `deploy/backup.sh` writes the volume's files into one archive, `indy-larry-state.tar.gz`, while the bot keeps running. The bot rewrites its files in place, so a copy can catch one half-written; each JSON file that doesn't parse is copied again, up to 10 times, and the backup fails if it never reads whole.
- `deploy/restore.sh <file>` takes a decrypted archive. It checks the archive first and changes nothing if it is damaged. Then it stops the bot, unpacks the archive inside the volume, swaps its files in for the old ones by renaming, and starts the bot again. If the swap fails part-way, the old files are put back before the bot starts. A restore killed part-way leaves `.restore-new` or `.restore-old` in the volume, and the next restore refuses to run until they have been checked and removed by hand.

To restore by hand on the VPS, as `deploy`, with an archive fetched and decrypted by the runner's helpers:

```bash
cd ~/apps/indy-larry && bash restore.sh /path/to/indy-larry-state.tar.gz
```

CI proves both on every pull request: `test/backup/roundtrip.sh` seeds a volume with the sample files in `test/backup/sample/`, backs it up five times while a stand-in bot rewrites `state.json`, wipes the volume, restores, and checks the bot starts with the backed-up state.
